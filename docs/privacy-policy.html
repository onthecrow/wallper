Wallper privacy policy

Effective October 29, 2026

Wallper is a live wallpaper app: you pick a video or a picture, crop it, and Wallper plays it on your home and lock screens. It also has a built-in animated black hole. This policy explains what Wallper keeps on your phone, what it sends, and why. It covers the Wallper app for Android, made by onthecrow.

In short

  • Your videos and pictures never leave your phone. Wallper edits and plays them on the device.
  • There's no account, no ads, and nothing is sold.
  • Wallper uses Google's Firebase to learn how the app is used (Analytics) and why it crashes (Crashlytics). Neither gets your media, your name or your precise location.
  • A report with the app's log is sent only when you tap «Send a report», and only to where you choose.

What stays on your phone

  • Your wallpapers. Wallper sees only the videos and pictures you pick in Android's photo picker; it has no access to the rest of your gallery. It copies what you pick into its own private storage, along with a thumbnail and, for an optimised video, the cropped and trimmed copy. Deleting a wallpaper in Wallper deletes its copies; the original in your gallery stays.
  • Your settings, such as how videos play, the theme and the black hole's look, are kept in the app's private storage.
  • Motion. The black hole turns as you move the phone, using its rotation sensor. Those readings are used on the spot and are never stored or sent.

Wallper keeps this data where Android makes it available before you unlock the phone after a restart, so the wallpaper shows right away. Other apps can't read it. Wallper isn't backed up to your Google account; moving to a new phone with a direct transfer may copy it. Uninstalling Wallper deletes all of it.

What Wallper sends, and why

Wallper uses two services of Firebase, run by Google LLC. They send data from your phone to Google's servers over an encrypted connection. Google processes it on Wallper's behalf, as described in Privacy and Security in Firebase and the Google Privacy Policy; Google's servers may be outside your country.

Google Analytics for Firebase

It tells Wallper how many people use the app, for how long and on which devices, so effort goes where it matters. It collects:

  • events such as the first start of the app, its sessions and how long it's in use;
  • the device model, Android version, language, app version, and an approximate country or region, which Google derives from the connection;
  • an app instance identifier, a random number that is reset when you reinstall the app;
  • how you came to the app, if Google Play tells it (the install referrer);
  • your device's advertising ID, if it has one and you haven't deleted it. Google uses it for summary reports such as age ranges and interests, and to measure ad campaigns for Wallper, if there are any. Wallper shows no ads. You can delete or reset the advertising ID in your phone's settings, usually under Privacy → Ads.

Firebase Crashlytics

When something goes wrong, Crashlytics tells Wallper what, so it can be fixed. It collects:

  • crash and error reports: where in the app the problem happened, and the app's state at that moment;
  • the app's own short notes before the problem, such as which wallpaper engine was running or that a file in the app's storage couldn't be played. They never contain your videos or pictures;
  • the device model, Android version, app version, the phone's graphics chip, free memory and storage, battery level, screen orientation, and whether the phone is rooted;
  • a random installation identifier, reset when you reinstall the app.

Wallper uses this data because it has a legitimate interest in keeping the app working and improving it. It isn't used to identify you, and it isn't combined with other data about you.

How long Google keeps it

Crashlytics keeps crash reports and their identifiers for 90 days. Analytics keeps event-level data for the retention period set in Wallper's Google Analytics property, 2 months by default; summary reports, which describe no single person, are kept longer.

Reports you send yourself

«Send a report» in the app's settings creates a text file with the app's version, your phone's manufacturer and model, its Android version and build, the time, and the app's recent log: up to 5,000 lines of Wallper's own messages, which may name files in the app's storage. Android's share sheet then opens, and you choose whether to send it and to whom, for example by email or in a messenger. Nothing is sent automatically. The file stays in the app's cache until the next report or until Android clears the cache.

What Wallper doesn't do

  • It doesn't ask for your name, email or phone number, and it has no account.
  • It doesn't read your location, contacts, camera, microphone or the rest of your gallery.
  • It doesn't show ads and doesn't sell or rent data.

Your choices

  • Delete any wallpaper in the app, or uninstall the app to delete everything it keeps on your phone.
  • Delete or reset your advertising ID in your phone's settings.
  • Ask for the data Firebase holds about your installation to be deleted, or ask anything about this policy, through the contacts below. Because Wallper doesn't know who you are, it may need you to help find your installation.

Depending on where you live, for example in the European Union or the United Kingdom, you may have the right to access, correct or delete your data, to object to its use, and to complain to your data protection authority.

Children

Wallper isn't directed at children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect data from them. If you think a child has used it and you want their data deleted, get in touch.

Changes

When Wallper starts to handle data differently, this page changes and its date above changes with it. The link in the app's settings always leads here.
